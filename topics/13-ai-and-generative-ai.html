Before we start

Why this matters

“My thesis is a ChatGPT chatbot.” “Lovely. What is the research question?” “…It chats.”

Generative AI made it easy to build impressive demos that answer no question. Good AI research measures reliability, compares methods fairly and explains the failures.

This chapter covers classical AI (search, planning, reasoning) and modern generative AI, with evaluation habits that make results credible.

What AI research studies

Artificial intelligence builds systems that perceive, reason, plan, learn and act. Machine learning is one part; AI also covers search and planning, knowledge representation and reasoning, agents, and today generative AI — large language models (LLMs) and image generators — with their evaluation and safety.

Part 3 of 12

Generative AI research, carefully

  • Prefer open models (e.g. Llama, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma families) you can run and fine-tune: results are reproducible.
  • If you use an API model, record the exact model version and date; outputs change over time.
  • Fix prompts, decoding settings (temperature) and run several times.
  • Build a test set with reference answers, written before you look at the outputs.
  • Combine automatic scores with human evaluation using a rubric and two raters.
A strong LLM thesis is a careful measurement or a clear improvement on a well-defined task — not a demo of a chatbot.

Methods

Key methods to know

Search

BFS, DFS, uniform-cost, A* with heuristics; local search; adversarial search (minimax).

Planning and reasoning

STRIPS/PDDL planners, constraint satisfaction, logic and probabilistic reasoning, Bayesian networks.

Reinforcement learning

Agents learning from rewards: Q-learning, policy gradients; also used to align LLMs (RLHF).

Transformers and LLMs

Pre-training, instruction tuning, prompting, in-context learning.

Adapting LLMs

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), fine-tuning with LoRA/QLoRA, tool use and agents.

Trustworthy AI

Explainability, uncertainty, robustness, fairness and safety evaluation.

Thesis ideas

StarterHallucination audit

Build a 300-question test set on a local topic and measure how often several open LLMs answer wrongly or invent facts.

StandardRAG for university documents

Retrieval-augmented question answering over course rules or policies; compare retrievers and measure faithfulness.

StandardLoRA fine-tuning a small model

Fine-tune a small open model for a narrow task (e.g. Bangla FAQ) and compare with a large model by accuracy and cost.

StarterHeuristic search for real maps

Compare A* heuristics for routing on an OpenStreetMap graph of Dhaka.

AmbitiousTool-using agents

An agent that uses a calculator, a database and search; measure task success and failure modes.

Thesis ideas

Getting started in four weeks

  1. Week 1AIMA chapters on search and agents; a Hugging Face tutorial
  2. Week 2run an open model locally; try prompting on your task
  3. Week 3build a small test set with reference answers
  4. Week 4measure two models on it; write a one-page idea
Pitfalls: evaluating on examples you looked at while designing prompts, unrecorded model versions, one run per prompt, trusting LLM-as-judge without checking it against humans.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

Turn “a chatbot for our department” into a research question.
Show the solution
“Does retrieval-augmented generation over the department’s official documents reduce unsupported answers of an open 8B-parameter LLM, compared with the LLM alone, on 300 student questions with reference answers?” — task, method, comparison and metric are now defined.

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

Your LLM scores 92% on a public benchmark released two years ago. Why be careful?
Show the solution
The benchmark may be in the model’s training data (contamination), so the score may reflect memorisation. Test on a new, private set written after the model’s training cut-off, or check for overlap.

Practice (1 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

1. Name four areas of AI beyond machine learning.
Show the answer
Search and planning; knowledge representation and reasoning; agents and multi-agent systems; natural language and vision; reinforcement learning; generative AI.
2. Why prefer open models for a thesis?
Show the answer
They can be run and fine-tuned locally, versions are fixed, results are reproducible and data stays private.
3. What is RAG?
Show the answer
Retrieval-augmented generation: retrieve relevant documents for a question and give them to the LLM so it answers from them.

Practice

Practice (2 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

4. Why does A* expand fewer nodes than BFS?
Show the answer
It uses a heuristic estimate of the remaining cost to explore promising nodes first; with an admissible heuristic it still finds an optimal path.
5. What is benchmark contamination?
Show the answer
When test data appeared in the model’s training data, inflating its score.
6. How do you make human evaluation of generated text reliable?
Show the answer
A written rubric, at least two raters, blind to the system, and a measure of agreement between them.

Summary

Summary

  • AI includes search, planning, reasoning, agents, reinforcement learning and generative models — not only ML.
  • Generative-AI theses need fixed model versions, private test sets, several runs and human evaluation.
  • Know RAG, LoRA fine-tuning, tool-using agents and classical search such as A*.
  • Watch for benchmark contamination and unvalidated LLM-as-judge scores.
  • Venues: AAAI, IJCAI, NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL/EMNLP, ICAPS, AAMAS.
  • A good AI thesis measures or improves something well defined — not a demo.
