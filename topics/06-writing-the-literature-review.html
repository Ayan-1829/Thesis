Before we start

Why this matters

Thirty paragraphs, each starting “X et al. proposed…”. The examiner sighs: “This is a shopping list, not a literature review.”

A literature review is an argument: here is what the field knows, here is how it fits together, and here is the missing piece — which my thesis supplies.

This chapter shows how to turn your notes and literature matrix into that argument.

Part 1 of 9

What a literature review is for

  • Shows you know the field: the key ideas, methods, datasets and results.
  • Organises earlier work so the reader sees its structure and trends.
  • Evaluates it critically: strengths, weaknesses, contradictions.
  • Ends with the gap — what is missing — which your thesis fills.
  • Justifies your choices of method, baselines, data and metrics.
A literature review is an argument that leads to your research question. It is not a list of paper summaries.

Part 2 of 9

Summary versus synthesis

Summary (weak)

“Rahman et al. [3] used an SVM and got 78% accuracy. Islam et al. [4] used an LSTM and got 82%. Das et al. [5] used BERT and got 88%.”

Paper by paper; the reader must find the pattern.

Synthesis (strong)

“Accuracy on Bangla sentiment rose from below 80% with feature-based models [3] to 82–88% with neural and transformer models [4, 5]. All three, however, use product reviews; none evaluates code-mixed social-media text, where informal spelling is common.”

Grouped, compared, and leading to a gap.

Ways to organise the review

Chronologicalthe history explains the present2010–2015 lexicons · 2016–2019 RNNs · 2020– transformers
By problem dimensionseveral aspects must be coveredAccuracy · Robustness · Efficiency · Fairness

Within each section, move from broad to specific: the general approach, the closest work to yours, and finally what is missing.

Structure

The shape of the review

Structure

The shape of the review (cont.)

  1. Introduce the scope: what the review covers and how papers were found (your search log).
  2. Sections by theme, each comparing and evaluating the work.
  3. Comparison table of the closest papers (from your literature matrix).
  4. Gap: one clear paragraph on what has not been done and why it matters.
  5. Bridge: how your research questions address the gap.

Part 7 of 9

Stating the gap

  • Not yet studied: no work has evaluated X on Y.
  • Contradiction: [4] and [7] report opposite effects of Z.
  • Limitation: existing methods need large labelled data, which low-resource languages lack.
  • Not compared fairly: methods were evaluated on different splits, so it is unclear which is better.
  • Context: results from English or Western hospitals may not transfer to local data.
“No one has done it” is not enough: also say why it matters that it is done.

Writing

Systematic versus narrative reviews

Narrative review

  • the usual thesis chapter
  • searches widely but informally
  • organised by the author’s judgement
  • faster; risk of missing work

Systematic review (SLR)

  • a research method in itself
  • protocol, search strings, criteria written in advance
  • PRISMA flow, quality assessment, data extraction
  • reproducible; takes months — can be a whole thesis

Even in a narrative review, describe your search and criteria briefly. It costs one paragraph and adds a lot of credibility.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

Rewrite as synthesis: “A [1] used CNNs on PlantVillage (99%). B [2] used ResNet on PlantVillage (99.5%). C [3] used MobileNet on field photos (81%).”
Show the solution
“On the lab-photo PlantVillage dataset, CNN models reach 99% accuracy or more [1, 2]. On field photos, however, accuracy falls to around 81% [3], suggesting that background, lighting and occlusion — absent in lab images — remain the main obstacle. Few studies test models trained in the lab on field images directly.”

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

Choose an organisation for a review on intrusion detection with three families of methods (signature-based, classical ML, deep learning) and two concerns (accuracy and explainability).
Show the solution
Methodological sections for the three families, each evaluated on accuracy, followed by a cross-cutting section on explainability, a comparison table, and the gap (e.g. deep models are accurate but rarely explained to analysts).

Practice

Practice (1 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

1. State three purposes of a literature review.
Show the answer
Show knowledge of the field; organise and critically evaluate earlier work; identify the gap and justify your research question and methods.
2. What is the difference between summary and synthesis?
Show the answer
Summary describes papers one by one; synthesis groups and compares them to reveal patterns, contradictions and gaps.
3. Name four ways to organise a review.
Show the answer
Thematic, methodological, chronological, by problem dimension.

Practice

Practice (2 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

4. Give three kinds of research gap.
Show the answer
Not yet studied in this setting; contradictory results; limitations of current methods; no fair comparison; results not tested in a local context.
5. Why is it wrong to compare 92% on dataset A with 85% on dataset B?
Show the answer
Different data (size, difficulty, splits, metrics) makes the numbers incomparable; compare only on the same benchmark and protocol.
6. What makes a review systematic?
Show the answer
A protocol written in advance: research questions, search strings, databases, inclusion/exclusion criteria, screening with PRISMA, quality assessment and structured data extraction.

Summary

Summary

  • A literature review is an argument that ends in the gap your thesis fills.
  • Synthesise: group, compare and evaluate papers instead of summarising them one by one.
  • Organise by theme (most common), method, time or problem dimension; go from broad to specific.
  • Use a comparison table to make the gap visible.
  • State the gap and why it matters; bridge to your research questions.
  • Describe your search briefly; a full systematic review is a method of its own.
