Before we start

Why this matters

A model reaches 99% on plant-disease photos taken in a lab. On a farmer’s phone in the field, it drops to 60%.

That gap — between benchmark images and the real world — is where much of the useful vision research for local problems lies. Closing or even measuring it well makes a strong thesis.

This chapter introduces image processing and computer vision: tasks, methods, data, evaluation and ideas.

Part 1 of 12

What vision and image-processing research studies

Image processing transforms images: denoising, enhancement, compression, edge detection. Computer vision extracts meaning: what is in the image, where, and what is happening in a video. Applications range from agriculture and medicine to traffic, document reading and robotics.

Part 3 of 12

The vision pipeline

  1. Imagescollect, check consent and licences
  2. Annotatelabels, boxes or masks
  3. Pre-processresize, normalise, augment
  4. Modelpretrained backbone + task head
  5. Traintransfer learning, then fine-tune
  6. Evaluateper-class metrics, held-out site
  7. Analysefailure images, Grad-CAM

Split by source (camera, field, patient, video) — not by individual frame — or near-identical images will leak into the test set.

Methods

Key methods to know

Classic image processing

Filtering and convolution, histograms, edge detection (Sobel, Canny), morphology, thresholding.

CNNs

ResNet, EfficientNet, MobileNet: convolutional feature extractors.

Vision transformers

ViT, Swin; self-supervised models like DINO; image–text models like CLIP.

Detection

Two-stage (Faster R-CNN) and one-stage (YOLO family, DETR).

Segmentation

U-Net, Mask R-CNN, and promptable “segment anything” models.

Generation

GANs and diffusion models — for synthesis and data augmentation.

Thesis ideas

StarterLab-to-field gap in plant disease detection

Train on PlantVillage, test on your own field photos; measure the drop and test augmentation.

StandardLocal vehicle detection

Annotate local vehicle types (rickshaw, CNG, leguna) and compare YOLO versions by mAP and speed.

StandardHandwritten Bangla OCR

Evaluate open OCR on handwritten forms; add synthetic training data.

StandardLow-light enhancement for detection

Does enhancing night images improve downstream detection?

AmbitiousEfficient on-device vision

Quantise and deploy a detector on a phone; measure accuracy, FPS and battery.

Thesis ideas

Getting started in four weeks

  1. Week 1CS231n notes on CNNs; OpenCV basics
  2. Week 2fine-tune a pretrained ResNet on a small dataset
  3. Week 3read 5 recent papers on your task (CVF Open Access)
  4. Week 4collect and label 200 pilot images; one-page idea
Pitfalls: frames of one video in both train and test, near-duplicate images, reporting only accuracy, no test on a new source, people’s faces without consent.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

You have 40 videos of traffic and extract 20,000 frames for detection. How do you split?
Show the solution
By video: e.g. 28 videos for training, 6 for validation, 6 for testing. Random frame splits put near-identical frames from the same video in train and test, inflating mAP.

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

A disease classifier is accurate but Grad-CAM shows it focuses on the background. What does that mean?
Show the solution
It may be using a shortcut (e.g. lab background or lighting that correlates with the label). Test on images with different backgrounds, crop or mask backgrounds, and add varied field images.

Practice

Practice (1 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

1. Distinguish image processing from computer vision.
Show the answer
Image processing transforms images (enhance, denoise, detect edges); computer vision extracts meaning (what and where objects are, what happens).
2. Name the metric for detection and for segmentation.
Show the answer
Detection: mAP (e.g. mAP@0.5:0.95). Segmentation: IoU or Dice.
3. What does a 3×3 convolution kernel do?
Show the answer
Computes each output pixel as a weighted sum of the 3×3 neighbourhood; different weights blur, sharpen or detect edges.

Practice

Practice (2 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

4. Why use transfer learning in vision?
Show the answer
Pretrained backbones already detect general features, so small datasets reach good accuracy with less data and training.
5. Name three top vision conferences.
Show the answer
CVPR, ICCV, ECCV.
6. How do you test robustness of a vision model?
Show the answer
Evaluate on images from a different source, and with controlled corruptions (blur, noise, low light, rain).

Summary

Summary

  • Image processing transforms images; computer vision extracts meaning from them.
  • Tasks: classification, detection, segmentation, OCR, restoration, video understanding.
  • Methods: classic filters and convolution, CNNs, vision transformers, YOLO/DETR detectors, U-Net, diffusion models.
  • Split by source, test on a new source, report mAP/IoU/Dice and show failures.
  • Venues: CVPR, ICCV, ECCV (free on CVF Open Access), plus WACV, BMVC, ICIP.
  • The lab-to-field gap is a rich source of thesis questions.
