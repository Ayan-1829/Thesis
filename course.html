Course outline

An interactive guide to undergraduate research in computer science — what it covers, how it is organised, and a typical plan for a one-year thesis.

General information

Who it is forFinal-year students starting a thesis, and anyone doing a first research project in computer science
StructurePart A (chapters 1–11): the research process, in order. Part B (chapters 12–21): one research field per chapter
Each chapterSlides with diagrams and interactive tools, worked examples, practice questions, a quiz and curated resources
OutputA thesis report, presentations and, ideally, a paper submitted to a conference or journal

What you will be able to do

  • Choose a feasible, interesting topic and turn it into precise research questions.
  • Search the literature systematically, judge the quality of sources and read papers efficiently.
  • Write a literature review that ends in a clear gap.
  • Design a methodology with data, baselines, metrics and a fair evaluation, and analyse results with statistics.
  • Work ethically: cite properly, protect participants and data, and use AI tools transparently.
  • Write, present and defend a thesis, and submit a paper.
  • Understand the problems, data, methods and venues of the main computer science research fields.

A typical one-year thesis

WhenWhatChapters
Months 1–2Choose the area, read widely, agree the topic with a supervisor1–5
Months 2–4Literature review, research questions, methodology; proposal defence2–7, 9
Months 4–8Data, implementation, experiments, analysis; progress presentation7–9
Months 8–11Write the thesis; revise with supervisor feedback10
Month 12Final defence; paper submission11

Adjust it to your department’s deadlines; the milestone checklists follow the same order.

How a thesis is usually assessed

  • Proposal: problem, questions, literature, methodology and plan (document and presentation).
  • Progress: work done, early results and the remaining plan.
  • Thesis report: quality of the research, analysis and writing.
  • Final defence: presentation and viva questions from the examiners.

Weights and forms vary between departments; follow your own department’s rules.
