1. What a Thesis Is and How It Works

1. Name three things that make a thesis different from a software project.
Show the answer
It answers a research question; it compares with existing work; it reports evidence from a planned method (and is judged on originality and rigour, not only on whether the software works).
2. List four kinds of contribution an undergraduate thesis can make.
Show the answer
A new method, a new dataset or benchmark, an empirical comparison of existing methods in a new setting, a system with evaluation, a survey, or a replication study.
3. Put the steps of the research cycle in order: analysis, question, writing, experiment, literature, method.
Show the answer
Question → literature → method → experiment → analysis → writing, and then back to new questions.
4. What should a weekly report to your supervisor contain?
Show the answer
What you did, what you learned (results, with numbers or plots), what blocked you, the plan for next week and the decisions you need.
5. Your experiments show your idea does not beat the baseline. Is the thesis failed?
Show the answer
No. A negative result that is measured fairly and explained (why it did not work, in which cases) is a valid contribution. Report it honestly and analyse it.
6. Why should you build a simple baseline in the first month?
Show the answer
It tests the data and the pipeline early, gives a number to beat, reveals problems while there is still time, and gives you something concrete to discuss with your supervisor.

2. Choosing a Topic and Research Questions

1. Name four sources of thesis topics.
Show the answer
Your interests, the future-work sections of recent papers, local problems and data, your supervisor’s projects, industry or NGO problems, replications of published results.
2. What four parts should a precise research question name?
Show the answer
The population or data, the intervention or method, the comparison, and the outcome (how success is measured).
3. Expand FINER.
Show the answer
Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant.
4. Improve: “Can machine learning predict exam results?”
Show the answer
For example: “Do features from LMS activity in the first four weeks predict which students will fail a course better than prior GPA alone, measured by recall of at-risk students?” — population, method, comparison and outcome are now explicit.
5. What is the difference between an objective and a contribution?
Show the answer
An objective is a planned step (“compare three models”); a contribution is a result you can claim afterwards (“we show that model A is 4 points better on code-mixed text”).
6. Why write down what is out of scope?
Show the answer
It protects the plan from growing, tells examiners you made a deliberate choice, and gives you natural future work.

3. Searching the Literature

1. Name three databases and say what each is best for.
Show the answer
For example: Google Scholar (widest coverage and citations), IEEE Xplore (networks, signal processing, IoT), arXiv (the newest ML/AI preprints); also ACM DL, DBLP, Semantic Scholar, PubMed.
2. What do AND, OR and quotes do in a search string?
Show the answer
AND requires all concepts (narrower); OR allows any synonym (wider); quotes keep an exact phrase.
3. Explain backward and forward snowballing.
Show the answer
Backward: follow a paper’s reference list to older work. Forward: follow “Cited by” to newer papers that build on it.
4. Why is a preprint on arXiv treated with care?
Show the answer
It has not necessarily been peer-reviewed; check whether a reviewed version was later published, and read its claims critically.
5. List four legal ways to get a paywalled paper.
Show the answer
University library access or proxy; arXiv or other open-access copy; author homepage or repository (Unpaywall finds it); emailing the author; inter-library request.
6. What should a search log record?
Show the answer
Date, database, exact query, filters, number of results, number kept — so the search can be repeated and described in the thesis.

4. Selecting and Judging Papers

1. Name the stages of screening.
Show the answer
Identify, remove duplicates, screen titles, screen abstracts, read full texts, include.
2. Give two inclusion and two exclusion criteria for a review of phishing-detection papers.
Show the answer
Include: detects phishing URLs or pages; reports quantitative evaluation. Exclude: only email spam (not phishing); no evaluation; predatory venue; duplicate version.
3. Where do you check a conference’s rank and a journal’s quartile?
Show the answer
Conference rank: the CORE portal. Journal quartile: SCImago (SJR), Scopus or Web of Science.
4. List four signs of a predatory venue.
Show the answer
Guaranteed or very fast acceptance; fees before review; spam invitations; very broad scope; fake impact factors; no verifiable editors or peer review.
5. Why is a high citation count not proof that a paper is correct?
Show the answer
Citations measure attention (and age); a paper can be cited often for being criticised or simply for being early. Correctness comes from method and evidence.
6. Can you cite a blog post or ChatGPT as evidence?
Show the answer
No. Use them for understanding or keywords, then find and cite the peer-reviewed source; verify every reference an AI tool gives you, because it can invent papers.

5. Reading Papers and Managing Notes

1. What do you read in pass 1?
Show the answer
Title, abstract, introduction, section headings, figures, conclusion (and glance at the references).
2. Which papers deserve pass 3?
Show the answer
Only the few your thesis builds on directly: your main baselines and the methods you extend.
3. Name five fields of a one-paper summary.
Show the answer
Citation, problem, method, data, results, limitations, relevance to my question, ideas.
4. Why must notes be in your own words?
Show the answer
Copied sentences end up in the thesis without quotation marks and become plagiarism; paraphrasing also tests your understanding.
5. What is a literature matrix?
Show the answer
A table with one row per paper and columns such as problem, data, method, metric, results and limitations; it shows patterns and gaps across papers.
6. Give three ways to understand a paper you find too hard.
Show the answer
Read a survey or tutorial first; run the authors’ code; watch the authors’ talk; discuss in a reading group; reread it several times.

6. Writing the Literature Review

1. State three purposes of a literature review.
Show the answer
Show knowledge of the field; organise and critically evaluate earlier work; identify the gap and justify your research question and methods.
2. What is the difference between summary and synthesis?
Show the answer
Summary describes papers one by one; synthesis groups and compares them to reveal patterns, contradictions and gaps.
3. Name four ways to organise a review.
Show the answer
Thematic, methodological, chronological, by problem dimension.
4. Give three kinds of research gap.
Show the answer
Not yet studied in this setting; contradictory results; limitations of current methods; no fair comparison; results not tested in a local context.
5. Why is it wrong to compare 92% on dataset A with 85% on dataset B?
Show the answer
Different data (size, difficulty, splits, metrics) makes the numbers incomparable; compare only on the same benchmark and protocol.
6. What makes a review systematic?
Show the answer
A protocol written in advance: research questions, search strings, databases, inclusion/exclusion criteria, screening with PRISMA, quality assessment and structured data extraction.

7. Designing Your Methodology

1. What are the main parts of a methodology?
Show the answer
Research design, data, pre-processing, proposed method, baselines, metrics, evaluation setup, and threats to validity.
2. Which design for: “How do software developers in Dhaka use code-review tools?”
Show the answer
A survey and/or interviews (qualitative or mixed methods), possibly with mining of repository data.
3. Name four kinds of baseline.
Show the answer
Trivial (majority/random), classic simple method, state of the art, ablations of your own method.
4. Why is accuracy a poor metric for imbalanced data?
Show the answer
Predicting the majority class gives high accuracy while missing the minority class completely; use macro-F1, recall, balanced accuracy or AUC-PR.
5. Explain external validity with an example.
Show the answer
Whether results generalise beyond the study: a model tested only on lab photos may fail on field photos; testing on a second, real-world dataset checks it.
6. List the sections of a research proposal.
Show the answer
Title and summary, introduction (problem, RQs, objectives), literature review and gap, methodology, expected contributions and risks, timeline, references.

8. Experiments, Statistics and Reproducibility

1. What is each split for: train, validation, test?
Show the answer
Train: fit the model. Validation: tune and select models. Test: report the final result, used once.
2. Give three examples of data leakage.
Show the answer
Duplicates across splits; the same patient/user in train and test; random splits of time series; normalisation fitted on all data; tuning on the test set.
3. Why run each experiment with several seeds?
Show the answer
Results vary with initialisation and data order; several runs give a mean and standard deviation and allow a significance test.
4. Which test compares two methods evaluated on the same 10 folds?
Show the answer
A paired t-test (or the Wilcoxon signed-rank test if the differences are not normally distributed).
5. What is the difference between an ablation study and error analysis?
Show the answer
Ablation removes components to measure their contribution; error analysis studies the mistakes to understand why the method fails.
6. List four things that make an experiment reproducible.
Show the answer
Code in Git with a tagged version; environment with versions; recorded seeds and configs; scripts that regenerate every table and figure; released data/models or an explanation; a README.

9. Research Ethics and Integrity

1. Define fabrication, falsification and plagiarism.
Show the answer
Fabrication: inventing data or results. Falsification: manipulating or selectively reporting data or results. Plagiarism: presenting others’ words, ideas, code or figures as your own.
2. Is changing a few words of a sentence and citing it acceptable?
Show the answer
No — that is patchwriting. Understand the idea, write it in your own structure and words, and cite it; or quote it exactly with quotation marks.
3. Give two acceptable and two unacceptable uses of AI tools.
Show the answer
Acceptable (if allowed): brainstorming search terms; grammar suggestions on your own text. Unacceptable: submitting generated text as yours; citing unverified AI-produced references; uploading personal data.
4. What does informed consent require?
Show the answer
Participants understand the purpose, what they will do, risks, how data is used and stored, and that participation is voluntary and they can withdraw; they agree before taking part.
5. What must you check before using a public dataset?
Show the answer
Its licence and terms (e.g. research only, attribution), any personal data it contains, and the paper to cite.
6. What is responsible disclosure?
Show the answer
Reporting a security vulnerability privately to the affected vendor and allowing time to fix it before publishing details.

10. Writing the Thesis

1. List the five main chapters of a typical thesis.
Show the answer
Introduction; literature review; methodology; results and discussion; conclusion and future work.
2. What goes in the introduction?
Show the answer
Context, problem with evidence, the gap, research questions, contributions, and an outline of the chapters.
3. Give the five moves of an abstract.
Show the answer
Context, problem, approach, main results (with numbers), conclusion/implication.
4. What is the difference between results and discussion?
Show the answer
Results report what happened (numbers, tables, figures); discussion explains why, compares with earlier work, and states the implications and limitations.
5. Three rules for figures and tables.
Show the answer
Numbered and captioned; referred to in the text before they appear; readable labels with units (and best values marked in tables).
6. Which chapters should you write first, and which last?
Show the answer
Methodology and results first (they are what you did); introduction, conclusion and abstract last (they describe what you found).

11. Presenting, Defending and Publishing

1. Name the four kinds of thesis presentation.
Show the answer
Proposal defence, progress presentation, final defence and poster session.
2. How long should each part of a 15-minute defence take?
Show the answer
About: problem and gap 3 min, method 3, results 5, analysis and limitations 2, conclusion 1, buffer 1.
3. What do you do if you cannot answer a viva question?
Show the answer
Say honestly that you do not know or did not test it, explain your best reasoning, and say how you would find out.
4. What makes a good poster?
Show the answer
A finding in the title, one clear message, large figures, little text (300–500 words), a logical why–how–what flow, readable from 2 metres, a QR code.
5. Conference or journal: give two differences.
Show the answer
Conferences: shorter papers, fixed deadlines, faster review, presented live, dominant in ML/AI. Journals: longer, several review rounds, slower, more weight in many fields.
6. What should be removed before releasing code publicly?
Show the answer
Personal data, credentials and API keys, large private datasets you may not share; and add a README and licence.

12. Machine Learning

1. Name four typical ML research problems.
Show the answer
Learning from few labels, robustness to distribution shift, efficiency, imbalanced/noisy data, interpretability, fairness.
2. What is overfitting and how do you detect it?
Show the answer
The model fits the training data (including noise) but generalises poorly; training error is low while validation error is high or rising.
3. Which model family should always be a baseline for tabular data?
Show the answer
Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost, LightGBM) — and a simple linear model.
4. Why use k-fold cross-validation?
Show the answer
With small data, it uses every example for testing once, giving a more reliable estimate with a variance.
5. Name three top ML conferences.
Show the answer
NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR.
6. What is transfer learning?
Show the answer
Reusing a model pre-trained on a large dataset and fine-tuning it on the target task.

13. Artificial Intelligence and Generative AI

1. Name four areas of AI beyond machine learning.
Show the answer
Search and planning; knowledge representation and reasoning; agents and multi-agent systems; natural language and vision; reinforcement learning; generative AI.
2. Why prefer open models for a thesis?
Show the answer
They can be run and fine-tuned locally, versions are fixed, results are reproducible and data stays private.
3. What is RAG?
Show the answer
Retrieval-augmented generation: retrieve relevant documents for a question and give them to the LLM so it answers from them.
4. Why does A* expand fewer nodes than BFS?
Show the answer
It uses a heuristic estimate of the remaining cost to explore promising nodes first; with an admissible heuristic it still finds an optimal path.
5. What is benchmark contamination?
Show the answer
When test data appeared in the model’s training data, inflating its score.
6. How do you make human evaluation of generated text reliable?
Show the answer
A written rubric, at least two raters, blind to the system, and a measure of agreement between them.

14. Natural Language Processing

1. Why is Bangla called low-resource?
Show the answer
It has far fewer labelled datasets, pretrained models and tools than English, relative to its number of speakers.
2. Name two Bangla-specific pre-processing issues.
Show the answer
Unicode normalisation of equivalent character sequences; conjuncts; mixed Bangla/English script; romanised (Banglish) text; spelling variation.
3. Which metric suits Bangla translation better than BLEU, and why?
Show the answer
chrF — character n-grams handle rich morphology better than word n-grams.
4. What is TF-IDF?
Show the answer
Term frequency × inverse document frequency: a word scores high if it is frequent in a document but rare across documents.
5. Name three NLP venues.
Show the answer
ACL, EMNLP, NAACL (also EACL, COLING, TACL).
6. What makes a new dataset credible?
Show the answer
Clear source and permission, annotation guidelines, two annotators with agreement, documented class balance, fixed splits, baselines, a datasheet.

15. Computer Vision and Image Processing

1. Distinguish image processing from computer vision.
Show the answer
Image processing transforms images (enhance, denoise, detect edges); computer vision extracts meaning (what and where objects are, what happens).
2. Name the metric for detection and for segmentation.
Show the answer
Detection: mAP (e.g. mAP@0.5:0.95). Segmentation: IoU or Dice.
3. What does a 3×3 convolution kernel do?
Show the answer
Computes each output pixel as a weighted sum of the 3×3 neighbourhood; different weights blur, sharpen or detect edges.
4. Why use transfer learning in vision?
Show the answer
Pretrained backbones already detect general features, so small datasets reach good accuracy with less data and training.
5. Name three top vision conferences.
Show the answer
CVPR, ICCV, ECCV.
6. How do you test robustness of a vision model?
Show the answer
Evaluate on images from a different source, and with controlled corruptions (blur, noise, low light, rain).

16. Cybersecurity

1. What is a threat model?
Show the answer
A statement of who the attacker is, their goals and capabilities, what is trusted, and what must be protected.
2. Why is the false-positive rate critical in intrusion detection?
Show the answer
Attacks are rare, so even a small false-positive rate creates huge numbers of false alarms that hide real attacks.
3. What legal rule applies to security testing?
Show the answer
Only test systems you own or have explicit written permission to test; use labs, VMs and CTFs.
4. What is the avalanche effect?
Show the answer
A small change in the input (one bit) changes about half of the output bits of a good hash or cipher.
5. Name the “big four” security conferences.
Show the answer
IEEE S&P, ACM CCS, USENIX Security, NDSS.
6. Why is NSL-KDD alone a weak choice of dataset?
Show the answer
It is old and has known artefacts; modern attacks and traffic differ, so results may not generalise. Use it only alongside newer datasets.

17. Biomedical and Health Informatics

1. Define sensitivity and specificity.
Show the answer
Sensitivity: share of truly sick patients the test detects. Specificity: share of truly healthy patients the test clears.
2. Why does PPV depend on prevalence?
Show the answer
With a rare disease, even a small false-positive rate among the many healthy people produces more false than true positives.
3. Why split by patient?
Show the answer
Several images or records from one patient in train and test let the model recognise the patient, inflating results.
4. What approvals does patient data need?
Show the answer
Ethics approval, de-identification, and a data-use agreement or credentialed access (e.g. PhysioNet for MIMIC).
5. Name two medical-imaging venues.
Show the answer
MICCAI, ISBI (journals: IEEE TMI, Medical Image Analysis).
6. What is calibration and why does it matter clinically?
Show the answer
Whether predicted probabilities match observed frequencies; doctors act on risk estimates, so a 30% prediction must mean about 30% risk.

18. Data Science, Data Mining and Information Retrieval

1. Name the six phases of CRISP-DM.
Show the answer
Business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modelling, evaluation, deployment.
2. How do you choose k in k-means?
Show the answer
Elbow method on within-cluster error, silhouette score, stability across runs, and interpretability.
3. What is BM25?
Show the answer
A classic ranking function scoring documents by term frequency, inverse document frequency and document length — a strong retrieval baseline.
4. Name two metrics for ranking quality.
Show the answer
nDCG, MRR (also precision@k, recall@k).
5. What is the cold-start problem?
Show the answer
Recommending for new users or items with little or no interaction history.
6. Name two data-mining and two IR venues.
Show the answer
Data mining: KDD, ICDM (also SDM, PAKDD, CIKM). IR: SIGIR, WWW (also WSDM, ECIR, RecSys).

19. IoT, Networks and Edge Computing

1. Name three trade-offs IoT research balances.
Show the answer
Latency, bandwidth, energy, cost, reliability, range.
2. Simulation or testbed: one advantage of each.
Show the answer
Simulation: large, cheap, repeatable scenarios. Testbed: real behaviour, noise and failures.
3. Why report the 99th-percentile latency?
Show the answer
Averages hide rare long delays that matter for real-time applications.
4. Name two IoT messaging protocols.
Show the answer
MQTT and CoAP.
5. What is edge computing?
Show the answer
Processing data near its source (on the device or a nearby server) instead of in a distant cloud, to cut latency and bandwidth.
6. Name two networking and two IoT venues.
Show the answer
Networking: SIGCOMM, INFOCOM (also NSDI, CoNEXT). IoT: SenSys, IPSN (also IEEE Internet of Things Journal).

20. Software Engineering Research

1. Name four research methods used in SE.
Show the answer
Controlled experiments, mining software repositories, tool building with benchmarks, surveys and interviews, case studies.
2. What is mutation testing?
Show the answer
Seeding small artificial faults (mutants) into code and measuring how many the test suite detects — the mutation score.
3. What is Defects4J used for?
Show the answer
A benchmark of real Java bugs with tests, used for testing, fault localisation and program-repair research.
4. Why filter forks and toy projects when mining GitHub?
Show the answer
They duplicate data or do not represent real development, biasing the results.
5. Name three top SE venues.
Show the answer
ICSE, ESEC/FSE, ASE (also ISSTA, MSR; journals TSE, TOSEM).
6. Give one risk in evaluating AI-generated code.
Show the answer
Passing tests does not prove correctness or security; benchmarks may be in training data; prompts and model versions must be recorded.

21. Human–Computer Interaction

1. Name four HCI research methods.
Show the answer
Interviews and observation, surveys, usability testing, controlled experiments, heuristic evaluation, field deployments.
2. How many users find most usability problems in a formative test?
Show the answer
About 5–8, tested in rounds; comparison experiments need more.
3. What does counterbalancing prevent?
Show the answer
Order effects (learning or fatigue) in within-subjects designs.
4. What is the SUS?
Show the answer
A 10-item questionnaire with 1–5 answers giving a usability score from 0 to 100.
5. Name two HCI venues.
Show the answer
ACM CHI, UIST (also CSCW, ASSETS, TOCHI).
6. Why is “users liked it” a weak result?
Show the answer
It is subjective, unmeasured and possibly biased; a study needs defined tasks, measures and analysis.
