Research question templates

  • Effect: Does X improve Y for Z compared with W?
  • Comparison: Which of A, B or C performs best for task on data, measured by metric?
  • Design: How can we build a system that does T under constraint C?
  • Explanation: Why does method M fail on case K?
  • Exploration: How do users U currently do task T, and what problems do they face?

Check it with FINER: Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant.

From topic to objectives

Area → topic → problem → research question(s) → hypothesis → objectives → contributions. Each step narrows the one before it.

Three-pass reading

  1. Pass 1 (5–10 min): title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusion, figures. Decide: read on or drop?
  2. Pass 2 (about 1 hour): figures, tables and the method; mark unknown references. Summarise in your own words.
  3. Pass 3 (hours): re-derive or re-implement; question every assumption; list strengths, weaknesses and ideas.

Questions to ask of every paper

  • What problem, and why does it matter?
  • What is new compared with earlier work?
  • What data, method, baselines and metrics?
  • Do the results support the claims? Any statistics?
  • What are the limitations and threats to validity?
  • What would I do next?

Thesis structure

  1. Front matter: title, declaration, approval, acknowledgements, abstract, contents, lists of figures and tables
  2. Introduction
  3. Literature review
  4. Methodology
  5. Results and discussion
  6. Conclusion and future work
  7. References
  8. Appendices

Abstract in five sentences

  1. Context: the field and why it matters.
  2. Problem: the gap or difficulty.
  3. Approach: what you did.
  4. Results: the main numbers.
  5. Conclusion: what it means.
