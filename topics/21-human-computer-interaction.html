Part 1 of 12

What HCI research studies

Human–computer interaction (HCI) studies how people use technology and designs technology that fits people: usable interfaces, accessible apps, new interaction techniques, and how technology affects communities. Its evidence comes mostly from people — observations, interviews, experiments and surveys.

The user-centred research process

  1. Understandinterviews, observation, surveys
  2. Definepersonas, tasks, requirements
  3. Ideatesketches, alternatives
  4. Prototypepaper → Figma → working app
  5. Evaluateusability tests, experiments
  6. Iterateimprove and test again

For research, the evaluation must be planned like an experiment: a question, participants, tasks, measures and analysis — not just “users liked it”.

Methods

Key methods to know

Interviews and observation

Semi-structured interviews, contextual inquiry; analysed with thematic analysis.

Surveys

Validated questionnaires (SUS, NASA-TLX, UEQ) and good question design.

Usability testing

Users perform tasks while thinking aloud; measure success, time and errors.

Controlled experiments

Compare designs A and B with enough participants; within- or between-subjects.

Heuristic evaluation

Experts check an interface against principles such as Nielsen’s ten heuristics.

Field deployment

Use in real life over weeks: logs plus interviews.

Part 7 of 12

Participants and ethics

  • Recruit participants who match the real users (not only classmates).
  • How many? 5–8 users find most usability problems in a formative test; comparisons need more — often 20+ per condition, or a power analysis.
  • Within-subjects designs: counterbalance the order of conditions.
  • Pilot your study with two people before running it.
People are your data: get informed consent, pay or thank participants fairly, protect recordings, and check whether ethics approval is required (chapter 9).

Combine numbers with why: quotes and observed problems explain the results. Use appropriate statistics for small samples (e.g. Wilcoxon, Mann–Whitney).

Thesis ideas

StarterUsability study of a local service

Test a popular local app or website with 8–10 users; report problems, SUS and redesign suggestions.

StarterAccessibility audit

Check local apps against WCAG and with screen-reader users; categorise barriers.

StandardBangla text-entry experiment

Compare keyboard layouts or voice input for speed and errors in a controlled study.

StandardTrust in AI advice

Experiment on how explanations change reliance on an AI assistant’s suggestions.

AmbitiousField deployment

Design, deploy and study a tool with a community (farmers, patients) over several weeks.

Thesis ideas

Getting started in four weeks

  1. Week 1Nielsen’s heuristics, SUS, thematic analysis basics
  2. Week 2observe or interview 3 target users
  3. Week 3read 5 recent CHI papers on your topic
  4. Week 4paper prototype + pilot test; one-page idea
Pitfalls: testing only with friends, leading questions, “users liked it” as the only result, too few participants for a comparison, no consent.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

Design a study comparing two layouts (A and B) of a payment screen.
Show the solution
Within-subjects experiment with 24 participants who use mobile banking; each completes 5 transfers with A and 5 with B, order counterbalanced. Measures: errors, task time, SUS for each layout, short interview. Analysis: Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on time and errors, thematic analysis of interviews. Consent and anonymised recordings.

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

Ten users give SUS answers; the mean SUS score is 58. What does it mean?
Show the solution
About 68 is the commonly cited average, so 58 is below average usability. Combine with observed problems (where users failed or hesitated) to explain why, and prioritise fixes.

Practice

Practice (1 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

1. Name four HCI research methods.
Show the answer
Interviews and observation, surveys, usability testing, controlled experiments, heuristic evaluation, field deployments.
2. How many users find most usability problems in a formative test?
Show the answer
About 5–8, tested in rounds; comparison experiments need more.
3. What does counterbalancing prevent?
Show the answer
Order effects (learning or fatigue) in within-subjects designs.

Show the answer

ACM CHI, UIST (also CSCW, ASSETS, TOCHI).

Summary

Summary

  • HCI studies how people use technology and designs technology that fits them.
  • Methods: interviews, observation, surveys, usability tests, experiments, heuristic evaluation, field deployments.
  • Plan studies like experiments: question, participants, tasks, measures, analysis; pilot first.
  • Measure effectiveness, efficiency and satisfaction (SUS), and explain the numbers with observations.
  • Participants need consent, fair treatment and privacy; recruit real target users.
  • Venues: CHI, UIST, CSCW, ASSETS, COMPASS; journals TOCHI, IJHCS.
