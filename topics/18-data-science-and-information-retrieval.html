Before we start

Why this matters

A shop owner has ten years of sales records on a dusty hard drive. “There must be gold in here,” he says. There is — mixed with a lot of sand.

Data-science research turns raw data into knowledge you can trust: patterns that hold up, rankings that serve users and forecasts tested honestly. Local data — sales, health, agriculture, transport — is full of good questions.

This chapter covers data mining, recommender systems and information retrieval.

Part 1 of 12

What data science research studies

Data science and data mining discover useful patterns in large datasets: clusters, associations, anomalies, trends and predictions. Information retrieval (IR) finds and ranks relevant items — web pages, products, documents — and powers search engines and recommender systems.

Part 3 of 12

The data-mining process (CRISP-DM)

  1. Business understandingwhat decision will the result support?
  2. Data understandingexplore, visualise, check quality
  3. Data preparationclean, join, engineer features
  4. Modellingtry several methods
  5. Evaluationagainst the goal, not only a metric
  6. Deploymentreport, dashboard or system

CRISP-DM is the most widely used process model for data projects. For a thesis, add a clear research question and a comparison with baselines at the modelling step.

Methods

Key methods to know

Exploratory analysis

Summaries, distributions, correlations and visualisation before any model.

Clustering

k-means, hierarchical clustering, DBSCAN; choosing k with the elbow or silhouette.

Association rules

Apriori, FP-growth: “customers who buy X also buy Y”.

Recommendation

Collaborative filtering, matrix factorisation, neural and sequence-aware recommenders.

Retrieval and ranking

BM25, dense retrieval with embeddings, learning to rank, re-ranking.

Time series and anomalies

ARIMA, Prophet, gradient boosting with lags; isolation forest, autoencoders.

Evaluation

TaskMetrics

RetrievalMRR, nDCG@10, recall@100

Thesis ideas

StarterCustomer or student segmentation

Cluster a real dataset, validate clusters, and describe them for decision makers.

StandardCold-start recommendation

Combine content features with collaborative filtering for new users or items; evaluate with a time split.

StandardBangla search

Build a test collection of Bangla queries and compare BM25 with dense retrievers.

StandardDisease or demand forecasting

Forecast weekly cases or sales with weather and holidays; compare statistical and ML models.

AmbitiousGraph anomaly detection

Detect fraud rings in a transaction graph with graph neural networks.

Thesis ideas

Getting started in four weeks

  1. Week 1pandas and visualisation refresher; CRISP-DM
  2. Week 2explore a dataset: quality report and 10 plots
  3. Week 3read 5 recent KDD/SIGIR/RecSys papers on your task
  4. Week 4baseline with a time-aware split; one-page idea
Pitfalls: skipping exploratory analysis, random splits of time-ordered data, uninterpretable clusters, comparing only against weak baselines, ignoring data quality.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

You cluster 5,000 customers with k-means and get 4 clusters. How do you make this research rather than a demo?
Show the solution
Justify k (silhouette, elbow, stability across seeds), describe each cluster with its feature profile, check stability over time (e.g. two years), compare with another method (hierarchical, GMM), and test whether the segments predict something useful (churn, spending) better than a baseline segmentation.

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

A recommender is evaluated by randomly hiding 20% of ratings. What is wrong?
Show the solution
Random hiding lets the model use future interactions to predict past ones. Split by time (train on earlier interactions, test on later ones) to match real use.

Practice

Practice (1 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

1. Name the six phases of CRISP-DM.
Show the answer
Business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modelling, evaluation, deployment.
2. How do you choose k in k-means?
Show the answer
Elbow method on within-cluster error, silhouette score, stability across runs, and interpretability.
3. What is BM25?
Show the answer
A classic ranking function scoring documents by term frequency, inverse document frequency and document length — a strong retrieval baseline.

Summary

Summary

  • Data science discovers patterns; information retrieval finds and ranks relevant items.
  • Follow CRISP-DM, with a research question and baselines at the modelling step.
  • Methods: clustering, association rules, recommenders, BM25 and dense retrieval, forecasting, anomaly and graph mining.
  • Evaluate with task metrics (silhouette, nDCG, MRR, MAE) and time-aware splits.
  • Interpret results for decision makers: clusters and patterns must make sense.
  • Venues: KDD, ICDM, CIKM, SIGIR, WWW, WSDM, RecSys.
