Glossary

56 terms used in the guide, in plain English, each linked to the chapter that explains it.

ABCDEFGHILMNPRSTVW

A

Ablation study
Removing or replacing one component of a method at a time and measuring the change, to show which part gives the improvement. Chapter 8 →
Abstract
A 150–300-word summary of the whole thesis: context, problem, approach, results with numbers, and conclusion. Written last. Chapter 10 →
Amdahl’s law
The overall speed-up from making part of a program faster is limited by the part that is not sped up. Chapter 22 →
Audit trail
The record of how qualitative analysis developed: interview guide, codebook, memos and every version of the themes. Chapter 23 →

B

Baseline
A simple or established method your proposed method is compared with. A fair baseline gets the same data and tuning budget. Chapter 7 →
Boolean search
A search string that combines terms with AND, OR and NOT, with quotation marks for phrases. Chapter 3 →
Bootstrap confidence interval
An interval found by resampling the test items with replacement many times and recomputing the result each time; the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles give a 95% interval. Chapter 8 →

C

Cache miss
An access to data that is not in the cache. Misses are compulsory (first access), capacity (cache too small) or conflict (blocks compete for one set). Chapter 22 →
Code (qualitative)
A short label attached to a meaningful segment of interview or text data. Chapter 23 →
Cohen’s d
An effect size: the difference between two means divided by a standard deviation. Chapter 8 →
Cohen’s κ (kappa)
Agreement between two annotators, corrected for the agreement expected by chance. Above about 0.6 is usually called substantial. Chapter 8 →
Confidence interval
The range of values for a result (such as a difference between two methods) that is compatible with the data, at a stated level such as 95%. Chapter 8 →
Confusion matrix
A table of true positives, false positives, false negatives and true negatives, from which precision, recall and other metrics are computed. Chapter 8 →
Contribution
What your thesis adds that did not exist before: a new method, dataset, tool, empirical finding, replication or analysis. Chapter 1 →
CORE ranking
A ranking of computer science conferences into A*, A, B and C. Chapter 4 →
Corrected resampled t-test
A version of the paired t-test for cross-validation folds (Nadeau and Bengio) that allows for the training data the folds share. Chapter 8 →
Cross-validation (k-fold)
Splitting data into k parts and testing on each part once while training on the rest; every item is tested exactly once. Chapter 8 →

D

Data leakage
Information from the test data reaching the model during training or tuning, which makes results look better than they are. Chapter 8 →

E

Effect size
How large a difference is, in the metric’s units (“+2 F1 points”) or in standardised form (Cohen’s d). Chapter 8 →
Error analysis
Studying a sample of a model’s mistakes, grouping them by cause and counting each group, to explain why the method fails. Chapter 8 →
External validity
Whether results hold beyond the data, people and settings that were studied. Chapter 7 →

F

Fabrication and falsification
Inventing data or results (fabrication); changing, selecting or hiding data or results (falsification). Both are research misconduct. Chapter 9 →
FINER
A check for research questions: Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant. Chapter 2 →

G

Gap
What existing research has not done, which your thesis addresses. The literature review ends by stating it. Chapter 6 →

H

Hypothesis
A testable prediction, for example “continued pre-training improves macro-F1 on code-mixed comments”. Chapter 2 →

I

Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Written rules for which papers (or participants) are included in a review (or study). Chapter 4 →
Informed consent
A participant’s voluntary agreement to take part after being told the purpose, procedure, risks and the right to stop. Chapter 9 →
Internal validity
Whether an observed effect is really caused by what you changed, and not by something else. Chapter 7 →

L

Literature matrix
A table with one row per paper and columns such as problem, data, method, results and limitations. Chapter 5 →

M

Macro-F1
The F1 score computed for each class and then averaged, so that small classes count as much as large ones. Chapter 8 →
McNemar’s test
A test for two classifiers on the same test set, based on the items on which they disagree. Chapter 8 →
Mixed methods
A study design that combines quantitative and qualitative data, such as a survey followed by interviews, and integrates the results. Chapter 23 →
Multiple comparisons
Running many tests raises the chance of a false positive; corrections such as Holm–Bonferroni adjust for it. Chapter 8 →

N

Negative result
A finding that a method does not help or a hypothesis does not hold. Well analysed, it is a valid answer to a research question. Chapter 24 →

P

p-value
If there were really no difference, the probability of seeing a difference at least as large as the one observed. It is not the probability that a hypothesis is true. Chapter 8 →
Paired t-test
A test of whether the mean difference between paired results (same seeds, same items) is zero. Assumes independent pairs and roughly normal differences. Chapter 8 →
Peer review
Evaluation of a paper by independent experts before it is published. Chapter 11 →
Plagiarism
Presenting someone else’s words, ideas, code or figures as your own without citation. Chapter 9 →
Planning fallacy
The tendency to underestimate how long your own tasks will take. Chapter 24 →
Precision and recall
Precision: the share of predicted positives that are correct. Recall: the share of real positives that are found. Chapter 8 →
Predatory venue
A journal or conference that charges authors but does no real peer review. Chapter 4 →
PRISMA
A standard flow diagram reporting how many papers were found, screened, read in full and included. Chapter 4 →
Purposive sampling
Choosing participants because they have the experience the study is about. Chapter 23 →

R

Reproducibility
Whether someone else can obtain your results again from your code, data and description. Chapter 8 →
Research question (RQ)
The precise question a thesis answers with evidence. Chapter 2 →

S

Saturation
The point in interviews at which new data adds few or no new ideas. Chapter 23 →
Seed (random)
The number that fixes a run’s random choices (initialisation, data order). Several seeds show how much results vary between runs. Chapter 8 →
Snowballing
Finding papers through the references of a paper (backward) and the papers that cite it (forward). Chapter 3 →
Stratified split
A split that keeps the same class proportions in train, validation and test. Chapter 8 →
Systematic review
A literature review that follows a written, repeatable search and selection protocol. Chapter 6 →

T

Thematic analysis
A method for finding patterns of meaning (themes) in qualitative data, usually in six phases. Chapter 23 →
Threats to validity
Reasons your conclusions might be wrong, and what you did to reduce them. Chapter 7 →
Three-pass reading
Reading a paper in three passes of increasing depth: overview, content, then full detail. Chapter 5 →
Train, validation and test sets
Data to fit the model, to tune and choose models, and to report the final result once. Chapter 8 →

V

Viva
The oral examination in which examiners question you about your thesis. Chapter 11 →

W

Wilcoxon signed-rank test
A paired test that uses ranks instead of values. With 5 pairs it can never reach p < 0.05; use at least 6. Chapter 8 →
