Before we start

Why this matters

Type “ami bhat khabo” into a translator and watch it panic. About 300 million people speak Bangla, yet the tools still stumble over Banglish.

That gap is your opportunity. A careful Bangla dataset, a fair comparison of models on real Bangla text or a method for code-mixed Banglish is a real contribution — one the big labs are unlikely to make.

This chapter maps NLP research, its methods, data and venues, with a focus on Bangla.

Part 1 of 12

What NLP research studies

Natural language processing (NLP) builds methods that let computers understand and generate human language: classify texts, extract information, translate, answer questions, summarise and converse. For a Bangladeshi student, Bangla is a low-resource language — fewer datasets and tools than English — so careful data work in Bangla is itself a valuable contribution.

Part 3 of 12

The NLP pipeline

  1. Collecttexts with permission; remove personal data
  2. NormaliseUnicode, punctuation, spelling variants
  3. Annotateguidelines, two annotators, κ
  4. Tokenisewords or subwords
  5. Modelbaseline → pretrained transformer
  6. Evaluatemacro-F1, BLEU/chrF, human checks
  7. Analyseerrors by phenomenon

Bangla needs attention at the first steps: Unicode normalisation (different code-point sequences for the same character), conjuncts, mixed scripts and romanised text.

Methods

Key methods to know

Classical text features

Bag of words, n-grams, TF-IDF with logistic regression or SVM — a fast, strong baseline.

Word embeddings

word2vec, fastText (good for Bangla morphology through subword units).

Pretrained transformers

BERT-style encoders (BanglaBERT, XLM-R, mBERT) for understanding; sequence-to-sequence models (mT5, BanglaT5) for generation.

Large language models

Prompting, few-shot learning, fine-tuning with LoRA, RAG.

Methods

Key methods to know (cont.)

Low-resource techniques

Transfer from related languages, data augmentation, back-translation, active learning.

Evaluation and analysis

Error analysis by phenomenon, probing, robustness to spelling variation.

Part 11 of 12

Thesis ideas

StarterA new labelled Bangla dataset

Collect and label 3,000–5,000 texts for an under-served task (e.g. cyberbullying, fake product reviews) with guidelines and agreement, plus baselines.

StandardBanglish (code-mixed) understanding

Measure how pretrained models handle romanised Bangla and test transliteration or continued pre-training.

StandardFaithful summarisation

Evaluate open models for Bangla news summarisation with a human faithfulness check.

StandardDomain question answering

RAG over Bangla textbooks or legal documents; compare retrievers.

AmbitiousDialect speech recognition

Collect regional-dialect speech, fine-tune an open ASR model and analyse errors.

Thesis ideas

Getting started in four weeks

  1. Week 1Jurafsky & Martin chapters on classification and transformers
  2. Week 2fine-tune BanglaBERT or XLM-R on a public Bangla task
  3. Week 3read 5 recent papers in the ACL Anthology on your task
  4. Week 4label a pilot of 200 examples; measure agreement; write a one-page idea
Pitfalls: no Unicode normalisation, duplicates across splits, accuracy on imbalanced classes, no annotation guide, personal data left in social-media text.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

You label 4,000 comments for hate speech with one annotator. Is that enough?
Show the solution
Not for a credible dataset. Write annotation guidelines, have a second annotator label a sample (e.g. 500), compute Cohen’s κ, resolve disagreements and refine the guide. Report κ and the class distribution.

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

Your Bangla classifier is 91% accurate, but 85% of comments are “not hate”. What do you report?
Show the solution
Macro-F1 and per-class precision/recall, especially for the hate class, plus the confusion matrix. Accuracy alone hides poor recall on the minority class.

Practice

Practice (1 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

1. Why is Bangla called low-resource?
Show the answer
It has far fewer labelled datasets, pretrained models and tools than English, relative to its number of speakers.
2. Name two Bangla-specific pre-processing issues.
Show the answer
Unicode normalisation of equivalent character sequences; conjuncts; mixed Bangla/English script; romanised (Banglish) text; spelling variation.
3. Which metric suits Bangla translation better than BLEU, and why?
Show the answer
chrF — character n-grams handle rich morphology better than word n-grams.

Practice

Practice (2 of 2)

Try each one yourself first, then check the answer.

4. What is TF-IDF?
Show the answer
Term frequency × inverse document frequency: a word scores high if it is frequent in a document but rare across documents.
5. Name three NLP venues.
Show the answer
ACL, EMNLP, NAACL (also EACL, COLING, TACL).
6. What makes a new dataset credible?
Show the answer
Clear source and permission, annotation guidelines, two annotators with agreement, documented class balance, fixed splits, baselines, a datasheet.

Summary

Summary

  • NLP covers classification, extraction, translation, QA, summarisation, dialogue and speech.
  • Bangla is low-resource: careful datasets and evaluations are real contributions.
  • Normalise Unicode, handle code-mixing, write annotation guidelines and measure agreement.
  • Methods range from TF-IDF baselines to pretrained transformers and LLMs.
  • Use task-appropriate metrics (macro-F1, entity F1, chrF, ROUGE, WER) plus human evaluation.
  • Venues: ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, COLING; read them free in the ACL Anthology.
